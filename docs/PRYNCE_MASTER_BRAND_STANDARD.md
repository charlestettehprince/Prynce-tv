# PRYNCE — Master Brand & Founder Identity Standard

**Brand:** PRYNCE  
**Product in this repository:** PRYNCE TV  
**Founder / Owner credit:** Prince Charles Tetteh  
**Tagline:** Beyond ordinary.  
**Home market:** Ghana  
**Core visual identity:** original Prynce logo and wordmark; deep navy / near-black, white and restrained gold.

This is the reusable brand rulebook for PRYNCE-owned projects. Apply it to websites, apps, media, documents, presentations, social graphics, product pages and launch materials.

## 1. Logo is the consistent identity anchor

- Use the owner's original approved logo and wordmark. In this repository the vector source files are `branding/prynce_icon.svg` and `branding/prynce_wordmark.svg`.
- Place the logo on the principal brand surfaces: website/app header or navigation, app launcher, opening/brand panel where appropriate, About/footer, official documents, share graphics and store/launch artwork.
- Preserve proportions, clear space and original shapes. Never stretch, crop important details, redraw, replace with a generic mark or recolour the source artwork.
- Use a small symbol-only version where space is tight; use the full wordmark when there is room.
- Keep the product's own identity clear (for example, PRYNCE TV) while visibly crediting the parent PRYNCE brand.

## 2. Founder photographs — use real owner-supplied originals

The owner's private Library contains these selected original portraits:
- `1000114846.png` — clean executive headshot.
- `1000118820.jpg` — navy-suit executive portrait.
- `1000119037.jpg` — seated casual professional portrait.

Use these originals as the founder-photo source set. Choose the crop that suits the layout; do not alter facial identity, facial structure, skin tone or defining features. Avoid heavy retouching, face replacement, synthetic face generation or misleading composites. Do not use a different person's face as a substitute.

Use the founder's real portrait strategically in:
- Founder / About the Owner profile;
- leadership, company overview and portfolio pages;
- official press kit, founder biography and project presentations;
- selected website hero or promotional creative when founder-led positioning is relevant.

Do not force a portrait into product icons, app launcher artwork, every content card, transaction screens or interfaces where it distracts from the task. The logo remains the primary persistent brand mark; the portrait establishes human ownership in appropriate founder-specific places.

Do not use family/child photos or food/product photos as a substitute for the founder portrait. Use project-specific imagery for project content.

## 3. Attribution

Where a founder or ownership credit is appropriate, use:
- **Prince Charles Tetteh**
- **Founder & Owner, PRYNCE**
- **Beyond ordinary.**

For PRYNCE TV, use a concise About/credits attribution such as “PRYNCE TV — a PRYNCE project. Founded by Prince Charles Tetteh.” Do not claim a broadcaster, partner or endorsement relationship that has not been confirmed.

## 4. Design system

- Primary palette: deep navy / near-black, white, restrained gold.
- Keep typography legible and consistent, with responsive layouts and sufficient contrast.
- Use original vector artwork for scalable brand surfaces; create correctly sized platform variants without changing the source mark.
- Use the logo consistently across phone, tablet, desktop and TV, respecting each platform's safe area and layout.
- Keep motion subtle and provide reduced-motion support where applicable.

## 5. Asset handling and publication

- The original founder portraits are in the owner's private ChatGPT Library; they are not currently committed to this public source repository.
- Do not copy private portrait files into a public repository, public CDN, public app package or public marketing page unless the owner has explicitly confirmed that the specific image may be published there.
- Until a portrait is approved for a particular public destination, use the logo and accurate founder-name attribution; never invent a replacement portrait or silently publish a private photo.
- Keep master artwork separate from project-specific exports. Record which source asset and crop were used for each published deliverable.
